# Brand Brief: Brolly Juniors

> *Where curiosity finds shelter.*
> Hyderabad · Classes 1–10 · Ages 4+

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Vision

### Core Mission

Indian parents are being sold two bad options for their child's future.

**Option one is the tuition centre**: 40 children to a room, a syllabus copied from the school, marks as the only proof of learning. It teaches compliance, not capability.

**Option two is the app**: a bright subscription that promises AI and coding, delivers a video and a streak counter, and never once knows the child's name.

Brolly Juniors solves the gap between them: **a child can be taught genuinely future-ready skills — AI, Python, phonics, abacus, mental maths, speaking, digital creation — by an adult who actually knows them, in a room small enough that they cannot hide.**

Batches are capped at **eight**. That number is not an operational detail; it is the entire product. Everything else — the 72-session annual curricula, the class-by-class ladder from Class 6 to Class 10, the six phonics levels, the ten abacus levels — is only deliverable *because* the room is small.

The mission, stated plainly: **to make a child's first encounter with a hard, modern skill feel safe enough that they try, and structured enough that they get good.**

### Value Proposition

> **Brolly Juniors is Hyderabad's learning home where children build real future-ready skills — AI, Python, phonics, abacus, Vedic maths and public speaking — in batches of eight, with a distinct curriculum for every single class, so no child is ever taught a lesson they have already outgrown.**

**Short forms for different surfaces:**

| Surface | Line |
|---|---|
| One-liner | Future-ready skills, taught in batches of eight, all under one trusted roof. |
| Parent-facing | Real AI and coding for your child — in a room where the teacher knows their name. |
| School-facing | An annual AI and coding curriculum, class-by-class, delivered or co-taught in your school. |
| Internal north star | Every child is seen, every week, by a person who remembers last week. |

### The Umbrella (the whole brand in one object)

The name is the strategy. An umbrella does three things at once, and each maps to a promise:

| The umbrella… | The brand promise |
|---|---|
| **Shelters** | Small batches. Safe to be wrong. Nobody is exposed. |
| **Covers many at once** | Seven programs, ages 4 to 16, one trusted roof. One relationship, not seven vendors. |
| **You go out *because* you have it** | Shelter is not shrinking. It is the reason the child ventures into hard things — AI, code, a stage, a microphone. |

**This third meaning is the one to protect.** Brolly is not a cocoon. It is the equipment that makes the storm survivable and therefore worth walking into. Every piece of communication should carry forward motion, not retreat.

## 2. Target Audience & Positioning

### Primary Persona — "The Informed Worrier"

**Meghana K., 38 · Hyderabad (Kondapur / Madhapur / Gachibowli belt) · Mother of Ishaan, Class 6**

**Profile.** Household income ₹18–35L. She or her husband works in IT, product, pharma or consulting. She is graduate-educated, reads about AI at work, and uses ChatGPT herself. She is on three school WhatsApp groups and one apartment-complex group, and both are her primary discovery channels.

**What she believes.** That the job market her son enters in 2036 will not resemble hers. That "learn to code" is already half-obsolete advice but "understand AI" is not. That schools are two to five years behind on this and will not catch up in time.

**Her pain points, in her own priority order:**

1. **"He's invisible in a class of 40."** Her deepest fear is not that her child is failing — it is that nobody would notice if he were.
2. **"I don't know if this is real or a scam."** The edtech collapse (Byju's, the aggressive-sales era) burned trust across this entire segment. She is now structurally suspicious of anything that sells hard.
3. **"Will he actually *do* anything?"** She has bought courses that produced a certificate and nothing else. She wants an artefact — a program that runs, a story read aloud, a talk given.
4. **"He already knows some of this."** She resents paying for a course that restarts at lesson one because it was built for the average of Classes 6–10.
5. **"It's already 4 hours of screen a day."** She wants the AI class to *earn* its screen time, not add to it.

**What flips her from browsing to booking:** the free trial class — but only because it lets her *watch the teaching*, not because it is free. She is not price-shopping. She is evidence-shopping.

**Why she chooses Brolly:** Three proofs, in this order —
1. **Eight children.** A specific, verifiable, expensive-to-fake number.
2. **A separate curriculum for Class 6 and Class 7 and Class 8 and Class 9 and Class 10.** Nobody builds five annual curricula unless they mean it. This is the single hardest claim for a competitor to copy.
3. **72 sessions across 36 weeks.** It is scoped like a school year, because it is meant to be taken as seriously as one.

### Secondary Personas

**The Early Parent — "Is my four-year-old behind?"**
Mother of a 4–6 year old, currently anxious about reading. Comparison-driven ("her cousin already reads"). Enters through **Phonics** or **Abacus**, not AI. Needs reassurance far more than ambition. She is the top of the funnel: a phonics parent at age 4 is an AI parent at age 11. *Nurture the relationship, not the transaction.*

**The School Decision-Maker — Principal / Academic Head / Correspondent**
Needs an AI and coding offering to stay competitive at admissions, and has neither the staff nor the curriculum to build one. Buys on: mapped curriculum documents, teacher-training support, structured delivery, and a partner who will not embarrass the school in front of parents. **Sells on credibility and paperwork, never on joy.** This is a different voice and a different set of collateral — treat it as a distinct brand register.

**The Child (the real user, and the one who renews you)**
Ages 4–16. Does not choose Brolly, but decides whether it survives. If the child resists on Tuesday evening, the parent cancels by Friday. The in-class experience — the progress dashboard, the module quizzes, the badges, the visible "I made this" artefact — exists to win the child, and winning the child is what makes the parent stay.

### Market Position

**The positioning statement:**

> For Hyderabad parents who want their child taught tomorrow's skills by someone who knows their child today, Brolly Juniors is the small-batch learning home that runs a distinct annual curriculum for every class — because unlike mass edtech platforms and neighbourhood tuition centres, we cap batches at eight and refuse to teach every child the same lesson.

**The competitive field:**

| Competitor | Their promise | Their real weakness | Brolly's wedge |
|---|---|---|---|
| **National edtech (WhiteHat-style, live 1:1 online)** | Scale, celebrity marketing, "your child will build an app" | Trust is destroyed. Scripted teachers, aggressive sales, no continuity. | A physical room in Hyderabad, and a curriculum that continues next year. |
| **Local tuition & abacus franchises** | Cheap, close by, familiar | Marks-focused, one-skill, no modern relevance, no pathway | Seven programs and a ten-year arc under one roof. |
| **Self-serve apps & YouTube** | Free or ₹500/mo, learn anytime | Nobody notices when the child stops | Eight children. Someone notices on the first missed Tuesday. |
| **School's own "computer class"** | Included, no extra cost | Two to five years behind, taught by whoever is free | The thing schools *license from us* — a partner, not a rival. |
| **Elite international curricula** | Prestige, global framing | Priced out, not local, not personal | Same seriousness, Hyderabad-rooted, family-priced. |

**The three defensible moats — lead with these, always:**
1. **Eight.** Specific, checkable, structurally expensive for a scale player to match.
2. **Five separate annual curricula (Class 6–10), for both AI and Python.** Ten distinct year-long programs. This is a *body of work*, not a course catalogue.
3. **Age 4 to Class 10 under one roof.** Nobody else in this segment holds a family for a decade.

**Explicitly do not compete on:** price, class volume, celebrity endorsement, guaranteed marks, or "your child will be a millionaire founder." Every one of those is a race Brolly loses and shouldn't enter.

## 3. Brand Identity & Voice

### The Core Emotional Angle

**Calm competence.**

The category screams. Edtech marketing runs on parental panic — *falling behind, last chance, the future is here, your child will be left out*. Brolly's entire differentiation is that **it does not raise the parent's heart rate.**

The emotional promise is the exhale a parent feels when they realise someone competent has this handled. Not excitement. **Relief with substance behind it.**

Every asset should pass the test: *does this make a worried parent calmer or more anxious?* If more anxious, it is off-brand, no matter how well it converts.

### Tone Pillars

#### 1. Warm — *the room is safe*
The default register. Human, unhurried, affectionate toward children without ever performing cuteness at them.

- ✅ **Do:** "Every new family can book one free trial class to understand the teaching approach and meet the educator."
- ✅ **Do:** Talk about children as people with football matches and grandmothers, not as "learners" or "users."
- ❌ **Don't:** Baby-talk, over-exclamation, or emoji-stacking in parent-facing copy. `🎉🚀✨ AMAZING KIDS!!!` is the opposite brand.
- ❌ **Don't:** Warmth as a substitute for information. Warm *and* specific, or it reads as evasion.

#### 2. Precise — *this is how we prove it*
Numbers do the persuading. Eight children. 72 sessions. 36 weeks. Six phonics levels. Ten abacus levels. Classes 6–10, separately.

- ✅ **Do:** "72 guided sessions: two one-hour sessions each week for 36 instructional weeks."
- ✅ **Do:** Name the real tools — ChatGPT, Claude, ElevenLabs, HeyGen — and name the fact that tools change.
- ❌ **Don't:** "World-class," "cutting-edge," "revolutionary," "best-in-class." Unfalsifiable adjectives are the vocabulary of the competitors this brand is defined against.
- ❌ **Don't:** Round numbers up or soften a limit. If a batch grows to nine, the brand has lied.

#### 3. Grounded — *no panic, no hype*
Never sell fear. Never over-promise the child's future. State what happens in the room.

- ✅ **Do:** "Coding is not a prerequisite." "Specific tools may change as safer options become available." Admitting limits *is* the trust mechanism.
- ✅ **Do:** Let outcomes stay proportionate — "reads a levelled storybook aloud and retells it in her own words" beats "unlocks unlimited potential."
- ❌ **Don't:** "Don't let your child fall behind." "The AI revolution won't wait." Scarcity, countdown timers, and last-seats-left urgency are permanently off-brand.
- ❌ **Don't:** Promise jobs, ranks, IQ points, or founder outcomes.

#### 4. Encouraging — *forward, not sheltered-in-place*
The umbrella exists so the child goes outside. Copy carries momentum: understand → practise → **create**.

- ✅ **Do:** Lead with what the child will *make*, do, and show. Verbs over adjectives.
- ✅ **Do:** Use the Explore → Build → Connect → Apply → Launch ladder as the shape of every progress story.
- ❌ **Don't:** Frame safety as protection *from* the world. Brolly teaches AI safety so children can use AI, not avoid it.
- ❌ **Don't:** Praise participation over outcome. "Visible outcomes" is a stated pillar — honour it.

### Voice Register Shifts

| Audience | Shift |
|---|---|
| **Parents** | All four pillars, warmth leading. Second person, short sentences. |
| **Schools** | Precise and grounded lead; warmth recedes to professional courtesy. Curriculum maps, delivery formats, teacher training. Never "joyful" in a school proposal. |
| **Children (in-product: quizzes, badges, progress)** | Encouraging leads, warmth close behind. Direct address, playful, never condescending. Celebrate the specific thing done, not the child's general brilliance. |

### Words the Brand Owns

`shelter` · `roof` · `home` · `eight` · `curiosity` · `seen` · `pathway` · `create` · `understand` · `practise` · `visible` · `joyful` · `trusted` · `future-ready`

### Words the Brand Refuses

`unlock` · `revolutionary` · `disrupt` · `10x` · `genius` · `prodigy` · `guaranteed` · `hurry` · `last chance` · `don't let your child…` · `rank` · `topper` · `world-class`

### Visual Anchor Ideas

The existing system in `src/styles/global.css` is already correct and on-strategy. This section codifies *why*, so it stays coherent as the brand extends into print, social and school collateral.

#### The Mark

**The umbrella is the logo. It should never become a generic education icon** (graduation cap, pencil, lightbulb, open book) — the entire brand rests on the umbrella's triple meaning.

Directions worth exploring:
- **The multi-panel canopy** — each panel in a different program colour (yellow, coral, mint, lavender, blue). Seven programs, one canopy, one roof. The strongest expression of "all under one roof," and it scales: a panel can be added when a program is.
- **The letterform umbrella** — the `J` of *Juniors* as the umbrella's handle/crook. Wordmark and symbol become inseparable; excellent at small sizes.
- **The umbrella as shelter, not object** — draw the *space underneath*: a curved canopy with figures beneath it. Warmer, harder to execute, best reserved for illustration rather than the primary mark.

**Construction rules:** 1.5px navy outline, hard offset shadow (no blur, no gradient), flat fills. The mark must survive being stamped on a certificate, embroidered on a T-shirt, and rendered at 16px as a favicon.

#### Colour

The palette is doing real strategic work: **navy structure holding joyful colour** — exactly the brand's argument that serious learning and joy are not in tension.

| Token | Hex | Role | Discipline |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cream** | `#FFF8E7` | Primary ground | The brand's calm. Warmer and less clinical than white — resist any pull toward pure `#FFFFFF`. |
| **Navy** | `#1E293B` | Structure, outlines, body text | The adult in the room. Every element is outlined in it — this is what stops the palette reading as a toy. Never a soft grey. |
| **Blue** | `#2B6CB0` | Trust, primary action | Buttons, links, technology programs. |
| **Yellow** | `#FFC93C` | Optimism, highlight | Highlights, badges, the umbrella's lead panel. Never for text on cream. |
| **Coral** | `#FF6B6B` | Warmth, human accent | Handwritten eyebrows, quote marks, the personal note. Use sparingly — it is the brand's heartbeat, not its bloodstream. |
| **Mint** | `#4ECDC4` | Growth, progress | Completion states, progress bars, achievement. |
| **Lavender** | `#A78BFA` | Imagination, creative programs | Art, storytelling, creative digital. |

**Colour discipline:**
- **No gradients, no blur, no glassmorphism.** Hard offset shadows only. The visual language should be *drawn*, not rendered — closer to a well-made children's book than a SaaS dashboard.
- **Bright colours are accents on cream, never large fills.** The moment three brights fight for the same area, the brand loses its calm and looks like a toy-shop shelf.
- **Assign a colour per program family** and hold it everywhere — site, worksheets, certificates, school decks. Colour becomes the child's navigation before they can read the label.

#### Typography

The four-family system is correct and should be enforced without exception:

| Family | Role | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Fredoka** | Display — `h1`–`h4`, logo, stat numbers, badges | Friendly, rounded, confident. Carries the warmth so the body copy doesn't have to. **Never for paragraphs.** |
| **Poppins** | UI chrome — nav, buttons, labels, chips, meta | Geometric and neutral. The competent, quiet layer. |
| **Inter** | Body — all paragraphs and long-form (400 only) | Chosen for reading, not for personality. Parents read a lot of this copy while deciding; make it effortless. |
| **Caveat** | Accents — eyebrows, hero script (coral, ~28px, rotated −2°) | **The single most protected element in the system.** A handwritten note, rotated slightly off-axis, is the visual signature of a human being present. **Use once per section, maximum. Never for information the reader needs — always for the aside, never the fact.** |

The −2° rotation matters: it is the deliberate imperfection that separates a *home* from an *institution*. Do not straighten it, and do not let it spread to other elements.

#### Illustration

Hand-authored flat SVGs in the brand palette, navy-outlined — no stock photography, no 3D renders, no AI-generated imagery.

**Why this is non-negotiable:** a brand teaching children to use AI *responsibly*, that generates its own marketing imagery with AI, is making an argument against itself. Hand-drawn illustration is a proof of care, and care is the product.

Children in illustrations should be **doing something** — building, reading aloud, presenting, sliding beads — never sitting passively and smiling.

#### Layout

- **Generous cream space.** Calm is spatial before it is verbal. Crowding is the visual form of anxiety.
- **Everything in outlined cards** with a hard offset shadow. The card *is* the room — bounded, safe, contained. This is the umbrella's logic applied to layout.
- **Numbers get scale.** `8`, `72`, `200+`, `1–10` set large in Fredoka are the most persuasive assets on the site. Let them be the loudest thing on the page.

## 4. Immediate Strategic Copy

### Taglines

**1. `Where curiosity finds shelter.` — the master line. Do not replace it.**
It is already in `site.js`, and it is the rare tagline that is simultaneously poetic, on-name, and strategically exact. It contains the whole brand: curiosity is the child's, shelter is the promise, and *finds* implies the child was already looking. Keep it as the permanent signature line under the logo and in the footer.

**2. `Eight children. One roof. Every future skill.`**
The proof-led variant, for performance marketing, ads and any surface where a sceptical parent lands cold. It leads with the number that cannot be faked and closes on scope. Use where trust must be established in three seconds.

**3. `Big skills, small rooms.`**
The compact, memorable variant — for merchandise, signage, WhatsApp status, sponsorship boards and any surface with severe character limits. Holds the entire positioning in four words and rewards a second read.

**Supporting lines by context:**
- *Schools:* `The AI curriculum your school hasn't had time to build.`
- *Phonics / early years:* `Reading begins with sounds, not spellings.` *(already in use — keep it)*
- *AI & Python:* `Each class moves forward — not back to lesson one.` *(already in use — keep it)*
- *Re-engagement:* `The umbrella is still up.`

## 5. Brand Guardrails — the one-page test

Before anything ships, it must pass all five:

1. **Does it make a worried parent calmer?** If it raises their heart rate to raise conversion, kill it.
2. **Is the persuasion a number or an adjective?** Numbers ship. Adjectives get rewritten.
3. **Would the claim survive a parent sitting in the room?** If a batch is nine, the site cannot say eight. Every claim is an operational commitment.
4. **Does the child come out looking like a *person*?** Named, specific, doing something — not a stock cheerful learner.
5. **Is the umbrella pointing outward?** Shelter that produces courage, never shelter that produces retreat.
