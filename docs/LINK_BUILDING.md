# Closing the link gap — 6 to 50 referring domains

The hard number: **littleinventors.in has 237 referring domains. You have 6.** That gap is the largest structural deficit on the site and the slowest thing to fix, which is why it starts now rather than in month three.

**Never buy links.** In a business selling to parents, a manual penalty would be terminal and there is no fast recovery. Everything below is earned.

Realistic target: **50 referring domains in 12 months.** That is roughly one a week. Most of them come from the first two sources.

---

## Source 1 — School partnerships (start here; you have already earned these)

You already run partnerships with Hyderabad schools. Every one of those schools has a website with a partners, programs or activities page. These are the highest-authority links available to you and you are owed them.

**Make it a clause in the partnership agreement**, alongside the logo usage clause: *"Each party will link to the other from its website."* Every future partnership then produces a link automatically.

For existing partners, ask. This email works because it asks for a specific thing in a specific place:

> **Subject:** Quick request — listing Brolly Juniors on your website
>
> Dear [Name],
>
> A small request. We are updating how our school partnerships are represented online, and I noticed the [Programs / Activities / Partners] page on the [School] website doesn't currently mention the AI and Python curriculum we run with your students.
>
> Would you be willing to add a line and a link? Something like:
>
> *"AI and Python curriculum delivered in partnership with [Brolly Juniors](https://brollyjuniors.com/schools)."*
>
> Happy to send a logo in whatever format your web team needs, and to write the paragraph if that is easier. We will of course list [School] on our side as well.
>
> Thank you,
> [Name] · Brolly Juniors · +91 81868 44555

**Also do the reverse.** Create a partner-schools section on `/schools` naming the schools you work with, with their permission. Reciprocal links between two genuinely related organisations are completely legitimate, and the page becomes a reason for the school to link back.

## Source 3 — Local press

Telangana Today, The Hans India, Deccan Chronicle Hyderabad, Siasat, and the Hyderabad desks of the nationals. Journalists do not publish "local centre teaches coding". They publish stories.

Real hooks you actually have:

| Hook | Why it is a story |
|---|---|
| **"Hyderabad children are learning AI safety before their teachers"** | Genuine, topical, slightly uncomfortable — which is what makes it publishable |
| **Original survey of your 200+ families** on screen time and AI use | Journalists cite data, and data with a local sample is rare |
| **A free AI-safety workshop at a government school** | Community angle, photographs, a named school |
| **A student project with a local outcome** | Human interest, and the child's name in the paper is worth more to that family than any discount |

## Source 4 — Free community workshops

A single free session produces three things at once: a link, a press mention, and enrolment enquiries. Nothing else on this list does that.

Where to run them:
- Apartment community halls in Kondapur, Gachibowli and Madhapur — reach out through MyGate or the residents' WhatsApp group
- Public libraries
- Government or low-fee private schools, which are far more receptive than the schools you would normally approach commercially
- Co-working spaces with parent members

The workshop that works: **"AI safety for parents — 45 minutes"**. Aimed at the parents, not the children. Almost every parent in the room is worried about this and almost nobody has explained it to them. The material already exists at `/resources/teach-kids-to-use-chatgpt-safely` — hand out the URL rather than a leaflet.

Ask the host to publish a line about it afterwards, with a link. Most will.

## Source 5 — Student project showcase

`/student-projects` is the most natural link magnet on the site, and it costs nothing to fill.

Publish real work: the Python game, the AI-made explainer video, the survey a Class 8 student ran. Then:

- Parents share it — that is social, not links, but it is how the page gets found
- Schools link to it when it is their student
- Local press ask for images when they are writing about it

One rule: **written parental consent for every child named or shown**, kept on file.

## Source 6 — The guides

The six guides at `/resources` are the only pages on the site anyone has an external reason to link to. A sales page never earns a link; an honest answer to "at what age should a child start phonics" does.

Use them actively:
- Answer questions in Hyderabad parenting groups with a genuine answer, and link the guide only where it adds something
- Send the AI-safety guide to school principals as a free resource for their parent newsletter — several will reproduce it with a credit
- Cite them in press pitches as evidence you have thought about the topic

## Source 7 — Original local research

The strongest long-term asset, and the one nobody else in this market will do.

Survey your 200+ families: screen time, AI use, what they worry about, what they have tried. Publish the findings as a page with charts. Anonymous, aggregate, with consent.

*"64% of Hyderabad parents say their child uses AI tools for homework. 11% have discussed how to check whether the answer is true."*

That is a statistic a journalist will quote, and a quote comes with a link. It is also, separately, genuinely useful information for your own teaching.

## What not to do

- **Never buy links**, including "guest post packages" and directory bundles sold by SEO agencies
- **No private blog networks**, no link exchanges at scale
- **No comment-spam links** on forums or blogs
- **No links from unrelated sites** — a link from a Hyderabad plumbing directory does nothing except look purchased
- **Do not chase Domain Authority alone.** One link from a school whose parents are your exact audience is worth more than five from directories nobody reads
