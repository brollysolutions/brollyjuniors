# Reviews playbook — 0 to 50 in six months

Local Pack ranking is driven more by review **quantity, velocity and recency** than by anything else you control. You have 200+ families. Fifty reviews is not ambitious; it is under-asking.

This document is the whole system: when to ask, what to say, what to do afterwards, and the mistakes that cost people their listing.

---

## The numbers

| Month | Target total | New that month |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 8 | 8 |
| 2 | 15 | 7 |
| 3 | 25 | 10 |
| 4 | 33 | 8 |
| 5 | 41 | 8 |
| 6 | **50** | 9 |

**Five to ten a month, steadily.** Not forty in a week.

> ⚠️ **The single most expensive mistake in this document.** A sudden burst of reviews is the classic signature of purchased reviews, and Google's filter is aggressive about it. The usual outcome is that the reviews are silently removed; the bad outcome is that the listing is suspended and you spend six weeks appealing. Steady beats fast, permanently.

---

## When to ask — the moment matters more than the wording

**Ask in person, at the end of a class, when the parent has just watched their child present something.**

That is the whole technique. A parent who has just seen their seven-year-old read a page aloud, or demo a Python game they wrote, is feeling something. Ask then, and they write two warm specific sentences. Ask by SMS on a Tuesday afternoon and you get a bare five stars, if anything.

Second-best moments, in order:

1. Immediately after a level completion or certificate
2. At the end of the first month, when the change is most visible to the parent
3. In the WhatsApp thread, within an hour of a good session — *not* as a broadcast

Worst: a bulk message to all 200 families. Low response, and the responses arrive all at once, which is exactly the burst pattern to avoid.

---

## The ask, word for word

Do not read this out. Learn the shape, then say it in your own words.

> "Can I ask you something? Most families find us by searching Google, and reviews are the main thing that decides whether they see us at all. If you have two minutes, would you mind leaving one? It genuinely makes a difference to a small centre like ours."
>
> *(hand over the card with the QR code)*
>
> "If you can, mention the program by name and which area you come from — that is what actually helps another parent decide."

Three things that make this work:

- **It explains why.** People help when they understand the mechanism.
- **It removes the friction.** The QR code goes straight to the review box. Every extra tap loses roughly 30% of people, and "search for us on Google" is four taps.
- **It coaches the content without dictating it.** Which brings us to the next section.

---

## Coach for specifics, never for words

A review saying *"Best abacus class in Kondapur — my daughter finished Level 3 in a year"* is worth ten that say *"Good teaching, nice staff."*

The specific one contains the program, the area and an outcome — the exact three things another parent is searching for, and the exact three things Google reads when deciding what your business is relevant for.

So prompt for the specifics:

- "Mention which program, if you can"
- "And which area you travel from"
- "And what changed for your child"

**Never provide the text.** Never write it for them. Never offer anything in exchange — no discount, no free session, no gift. Review gating and incentivised reviews violate Google's policies, and in an education business aimed at parents, being caught doing it is worse than the ranking you would have gained.

Generate the QR from the direct review link in your GBP dashboard (**Ask for reviews** → copy link). Test it on two phones before printing 50.

## Replying — do this within 48 hours, every time

Replies are indexed. They are a second chance to state what the business does, in the words a searcher uses. A review that says "great classes" plus a reply that says "so glad Aarvi is enjoying Vedic Maths" is a far stronger relevance signal than the review alone.

Rules:
- Reply to **every** review, including the one-word five-stars
- Name the child (they already did) and **name the program**
- Two or three sentences. A paragraph reads as marketing
- Never copy-paste the same reply twice — Google can see that, and so can parents

### Templates to adapt, not to paste

**Warm and specific (5★)**
> Thank you, Anitha. It has been a pleasure watching Aarvi's confidence with Vedic Maths grow this term — she explained a shortcut to the whole batch last week. See you Saturday.

**Short and generic (5★, no detail)**
> Thank you, Vikram — glad Reyansh is enjoying the Python sessions. Do tell us if there is anything you would like to see more of.

**Mentions a program by name**
> Thank you. Phonics Level 4 is the stage where it usually clicks, and it clearly has for Ishaan. Thank you for the weekly practice at home — it makes a visible difference.

**A 4★ with a small criticism**
> Thank you for saying so directly — that is genuinely useful. You are right that the Saturday batch has been running slightly over, and we have changed the timings from this week. Please tell us if it does not improve.

**A 1–2★, whether fair or not**
> Thank you for telling us, and I am sorry the experience did not match what you expected. I would like to understand what happened properly — could you call me on +91 70360 44555? I will look into it either way.

### The rules for a bad review

1. **Reply within 24 hours.** Slower looks like avoidance.
2. **Never argue, never explain in public, never mention the child by name.**
3. **Move it to a phone call.** Everything useful happens off the page.
4. **Never mention fees, attendance or anything personal about the family.** Even when they did.
5. If it is defamatory, off-topic, or clearly not from a customer, report it — but reply first. A reported review can take weeks and may not be removed; an unanswered one-star sits there permanently.

One negative review among twenty positives costs you almost nothing, and a profile of nothing but five stars reads as fake to parents. A well-handled criticism is genuinely persuasive.

## Tracking

Keep this in a spreadsheet. Update it on the same day each week.

| Week | New reviews | Total | Average | Asked (count) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | |

If **asked** is much higher than **new**, the problem is friction — check the QR link works, and check you are asking at the end of class rather than by message.

If **asked** is low, the problem is that nobody remembers to ask. Put it in the class close-out routine, not in someone's memory.
